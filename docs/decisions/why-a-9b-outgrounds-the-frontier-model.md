# Why a 9B model outgrounds the frontier model on ScreenSpot

Our measured ScreenSpot table has the local arms above `claude-sonnet-5`, which reads as implausible
until you look at what each vendor trained for. It is not a general capability gap. It is a
task-specific training gap, and the mechanism is documented on both sides.

| arm | ScreenSpot 1272, single turn, native images |
| --- | --- |
| claude-sonnet-5 | 68.3% |
| base Qwen3.5-9B | 72.2% |
| cua-sft-v2 | 85.4% |
| cua-grpo-v3 | 85.1% |
| cua-sft-v3 | 91.6% |

## Qwen trains grounding as a named objective, with purpose-built data

The Qwen3-VL technical report (arXiv 2511.21631) devotes a data section to it — §3.2.4 *Grounding and
Counting*: *"we systematically enhance grounding proficiency and support two grounding modalities:
bounding boxes and points."*

- **Box grounding** aggregates COCO, Objects365, OpenImages and RefCOCO/+/g, then adds an automated
  synthesis pipeline: candidates from Qwen2.5-VL, localisation by Grounding DINO, then confidence
  filtering, yielding *"a large-scale, highly diverse box-based grounding dataset."*
- **Point grounding** — the exact ScreenSpot task, a single click point — combines PixMo pointing
  annotations, detection/segmentation-derived data, and *"high-precision pointing annotations
  generated by a dedicated synthesis pipeline designed to target fine-grained image details."*
- §3.2.8 adds **one million more point-grounding samples** from programmatically rendered diagrams:
  intersection points, corners, centres of gravity.

## And it trains on GUI screenshots in exactly ScreenSpot's three domains

§3.2.9 *Agent / GUI*: *"we curate and synthesize large-scale, cross-platform data spanning desktop,
mobile, and web environments... element description, dense captioning, and dense grounding."*

ScreenSpot's eight sources are mobile (android, ios), desktop (macos, windows) and web (gitlab, shop,
tool, forum). The same three domains, named.

## The output convention in our registry is Qwen's, not ours

*"Different from Qwen2.5-VL, we adopt a normalized coordinate system scaled to the range [0, 1000]."*

That sentence is why `normalized_1000` exists as a `coord_space` and why the untuned base answers in
it without being asked. It also explains the withdrawn 36.5% frontier row: prompting for 0-1000 asked
the frontier model to adopt a convention Qwen was trained on and Anthropic explicitly advises against
(*"Claude does not work well when you ask for normalized coordinates... Always ask for pixel
coordinates"*, Anthropic vision-coordinates guide).

## Anthropic optimises the same capability differently

Anthropic's vision documentation states that *"Claude's coordinate and localization outputs are
approximate"*, and its recommended technique for fine targets is a scaffold, not a single answer:
*"crop the region of interest and send the crop (offset returned coordinates by the crop origin), or
use a high-resolution-tier model."* Computer use ships as a tool where that refinement lives in the
harness. Precise absolute pixels from one look at a full screenshot is not the interface the model is
built to serve, so it is not what it is tuned for.

## The ordering is protocol-dependent, and reverses on ScreenSpot-Pro

This must be stated, because a reviewer who has seen the leaderboards will otherwise catch it:

| benchmark | best Qwen3-VL (self-reported) | Claude (leaderboard) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ScreenSpot-Pro | 61.8 / 62.0% | Opus 4.8 **87.9%**, Opus 4.6 83.1%, Opus 4.5 45.7% |

On the harder professional-resolution set, published Claude figures **beat** published Qwen figures.
Those runs are not single-turn: the leaderboard's own FAQ warns that *"cropping, visual search,
planners, Python tools, image resolution, and decoding policy can materially change the result."*
Sonnet 5 does not appear on that board at all.

So the honest claim is narrow and defensible: **under one bare single-turn protocol on standard
ScreenSpot, GUI-tuned Qwen checkpoints ground better than Sonnet 5.** It is not "a 9B beats the
frontier at grounding" in general.

## Why this supports the thesis rather than undermining it

The offloading argument never needed the small model to be smarter. It needed exactly this split,
and the two lanes together are the result:

| | grounding (perception) | QA bench DDR (judgement) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| claude-sonnet-5 | 68.3% | **66.7%** |
| cua-sft-v2 | **85.4%** | 9.1% |
| cua-sft-v3 | **91.6%** | 21.2% |
| cua-grpo-v3 | 85.1% | 24.2% |

Perception is narrow, heavily supervisable, and a small specialist wins it. Deciding whether what you
see is a defect is not, and the frontier model keeps a 3x lead. That is the argument for a hybrid
where the local model executes and the frontier model concludes, and it is why the economic claim
rests on the QA bench.

## The limitation to declare before someone asks

ScreenSpot predates Qwen3-VL, and Qwen trains on GUI screenshots with dense grounding annotations
across the same three domains. Whether ScreenSpot's images, or the public corpora they were drawn
from, sit inside that training set cannot be determined from outside. The base model's 72.2% may
therefore partly reflect familiarity with the distribution rather than transferable skill, and the
same caveat carries to every LoRA trained on top of it.

This does not weaken the before/after deltas the thesis rests on, which compare checkpoints sharing
one base through one harness. It does mean the base-versus-frontier row is a comparison between a
model that may have seen this distribution and one that has not, and it should be reported that way.
